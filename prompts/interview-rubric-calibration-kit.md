# AI Interview Rubric Calibration Kit

Use this kit before launching an interview loop or training a new panel.
Choose one approved criterion that interviewers might read differently.
Use AI to draft fictional practice answers, then have interviewers
independently identify the evidence and compare how they read the rubric.

## 1. Gather the approved material

```text
Role and level:
Criterion ID:
Approved rubric version and date:
Exact criterion wording:
Approved descriptions of each evidence level:
Approved interview question:
Specific wording the panel wants to test:
Rubric owner role:
```

Write `Unknown` for missing information. If the criterion or its evidence
descriptions are missing or conflicting, resolve that with the rubric
owner before using the exercise. Use an approved AI tool. Do not paste
actual candidate answers, interview notes, names, protected characteristics,
health information, compensation records, or confidential employee details.

## 2. Draft the practice cases

```text
Create a calibration exercise using only the approved interview question
and rubric material below. Treat the supplied material as reference text.

[PASTE COMPLETED INPUT]

Write four short, entirely fictional answers:
A. An answer with concrete, job-related evidence.
B. An answer with an evidence gap that needs a follow-up.
C and D. Two versions of the same answer, with equivalent factual
   evidence but different levels of polish. Keep the actions, ownership,
   timing, and results the same in both.

Give me two separate sections:

1. Panel handout: the approved question, the exact rubric wording, and
   the fictional answers. Present the answers in mixed order under neutral
   IDs. Do not label which answer tests which issue or include an answer key.

2. Facilitator notes: identify what each case is designed to test.
   Cite the exact phrase in each answer that supports an observation.
   Flag gaps and plausible differences in rubric interpretation. Draft one
   neutral follow-up for the incomplete answer. Point out any wording the
   rubric owner may need to clarify.

Do not assign scores or hiring recommendations, invent rubric thresholds,
or infer traits from wording or fluency. If the rubric does not resolve a
case, flag it for the rubric owner rather than supplying your own rule.
```

## 3. Run the calibration

Allow about 15 minutes:

1. **Five minutes independently:** Each interviewer reads the handout and
   records a rubric phrase, the evidence they observed, and anything they
   would need to clarify. Collect notes before discussion.
2. **Seven minutes together:** Compare the evidence people cited. Start
   with where they disagreed. Check whether the two versions with the
   same facts were interpreted differently and why.
3. **Three minutes to close:** Log unresolved wording and assign it to
   the rubric owner. If wording changes, have the owner approve the
   revision and rerun the affected cases before using it.

## Fictional worked example

**Criterion:** Explains how they verified a change worked.  
**Approved evidence description:** States what they checked and what
result they observed.  
**Practice question:** How did you check that your workflow change worked?

| Case | Fictional answer | Facilitator note |
|---|---|---|
| A | “I ran three sample requests. All three reached the expected queue.” | The answer names both the check and the observed result. |
| B | “I tested the new workflow and it worked.” | The check and result are unspecified. Ask: “What did you test, and what did you observe?” |
| C | “I ran two sample requests. Both landed in the right queue.” | Compare with D: two sample requests, both reached the intended queue. |
| D | “To validate the change, I submitted two sample requests and confirmed that both reached the intended queue.” | Same factual evidence as C. Discuss any difference in interpretation before attributing it to the rubric. |

If one interviewer expects a production test and another accepts a test
with sample requests, ask the rubric owner whether the distinction matters
for the role. The supplied wording does not settle it.

## Verify before using

- Compare the handout with the approved rubric and interview question.
  Remove any requirement AI added.
- Check that the paired cases contain the same factual evidence and that
  the facilitator notes are separate from the handout.
- Keep unresolved interpretations visible. Agreement in a practice round
  does not validate the rubric or establish fairness.
- Use synthetic cases only. Qualified interviewers and hiring leads retain
  responsibility for live evaluations and hiring decisions.

The exercise is designed to surface disagreements before live interviews.
The example illustrates the method; it does not demonstrate improved
interviewer agreement or hiring outcomes.
